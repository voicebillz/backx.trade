Every trade has two sides: someone taking a position, and someone providing the capital that makes it possible.

On BackX both sides can be humans or AI agents.

• Issuers put USDC into a coin's pool. That capital backs traders and earns from their activity.
• Traders open positions backed by that pool.
• Everything settles on-chain in open contracts. No hidden book, no off-chain matching engine, no "trust us".

An agent can be an issuer, a trader, or both. It can read the pool, the positions and the liquidation prices straight from the chain and decide where its capital works best.

𝗠𝘂𝗹𝘁𝗶𝗽𝗹𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻, 𝗰𝗮𝗽𝗽𝗲𝗱 𝗮𝘁 𝟮𝟬×

BackX has Multiplication trading, also known as leverage.

It is capped at 20×. On purpose.

Here is the math. On BackX the amount you type is what you pay: fees come out of it, the rest is your margin, and issuers keep a small safety margin so they get their capital back first. Run the numbers on a major coin:

• 5× → liquidation at −12.1 %
• 10× → liquidation at −6.1 %
• 20× → liquidation at −2.1 %
• 50× → liquidated the moment it opens
• 100× → liquidated the moment it opens

Even with zero safety margin, 50× survives a 0.15 % move. That is one tick. That is not a trade.

[IMAGE 2: brand/marketing/article/2-capped-20x.png]

For an AI agent that runs on math and probabilities, there is no sensible equation that defends going above 20×. Past that point the outcome is decided by noise, not by the idea. For the trader it becomes a coin flip. For the issuer backing it, it becomes a full-range gamble on the next candle.

That is not what BackX is about. BackX is built for capital that wants to be right, not lucky.

𝗢𝗹𝗱-𝘀𝗰𝗵𝗼𝗼𝗹 𝘀𝗵𝗼𝗿𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴, 𝗯𝗮𝗰𝗸 𝗼𝗻-𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗶𝗻

BackX will bring back real short mechanics.

Not a synthetic "short" against a funding rate. The real thing: you literally borrow the asset (stocks, memes, any listed token) from an agent or an issuer pool, sell it, and buy it back later to return it.

[IMAGE 3: brand/marketing/article/3-short-borrow.png]

Here is why that works for issuers:

• Issuers who lend the asset always get paid in USDC.
• Their risk is always the same: price down.
• So why not lend it to agents and traders, and earn steady borrow fees in USDC whether the price goes up or down?

Holders become lenders. Agents get the inventory they need to hedge, short and market-make. The book stays on-chain, where everyone can see who borrowed what.

A market is only as safe as the thing that liquidates it.

BackX will run on-chain validators across all our trading chains. Validators check every position, settle liquidations and fill orders, and BackX allocates a share of the rewards to them.

• Anyone can be a validator.
• We build it so AI agents can be validators too.
• If our own servers go down, the market keeps running. Liquidations, limit fills and issuer withdrawals never depend on us.
